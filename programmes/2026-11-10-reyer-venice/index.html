Two defeats in four days, one by two points in overtime in Rome and one by 21 at home to Leicester, bring Umana Reyer Venezia to the Copper Box with the Lions on 3–3 in EuroCup group B. Venice are second in the group on the same record, and arrive from a win in Italy's league on Saturday.

Form two narrow chances, then a heavy night

The Lions beat Bristol 82–76 on 24 October, Turk Telekom Ankara 79–61 on 28 October and Sheffield 85–76 on 30 October. Then came Rome. London led 44–42 at half-time, trailed by as many as 13 in the third quarter, and tied it at 76 when Maxwell Lewis converted an and-one with 25.3 seconds left, before losing 86–84 in overtime. Head coach Tautvydas Sabonis pointed at the line: “We missed some open shots, and if I'm not mistaken, we missed 15 free throws, maybe even 20 free throws, in a game you lose in overtime.” The Lions finished 10-for-23 from the line.

On Friday Leicester Riders won 97–76 at NCC Hackney. The Lions led 22–20 after a quarter and trailed 41–39 at the break, but a 70–57 deficit going into the fourth became a 23-point loss. Leicester shot 50% from the field and scored 16 second-chance points; Thomas Kennedy's 20 points on 8-12 shooting was the Lions' best.

Reyer Venice second, 3–3

Neven Spahija's side have lost three of six in the group but beat Siauliai 98–77 at the Taliercio on 4 November, leading throughout and by as many as 25. Before that they lost 82–80 at La Laguna Tenerife and 90–79 at Maxima Roma, and beat Turk Telekom Ankara 75–65 at home. Reyer's report on the Siauliai game says they remain unbeaten at home in Europe, and that six players reached double figures. On Saturday they won 83–78 at Reggio Emilia in the Italian league after trailing by as many as 14, with Kyle Wiltjer scoring three consecutive baskets in the last minute and a half.

Spahija's own words after that win were about consistency: his side need to be steady for all 40 minutes, he said, and their identity has to start from defence.
